# Participant guide

Read the common contract, your complete family text and common packet. Independently author a task-specific investigative question preserving the family's concern and the user's original request. Use constructive and challenge prompts. Name one missing assumption or perspective that could change the next action; a lens name alone does not demonstrate a different contribution.

## Initial commitment

Provide a concise brief with a framing or mechanism, its dependency, a concrete specimen, evidence separated from analogy/conjecture, strongest objection and distinguishing test with a disconfirming result. A specimen can be an interaction, decision rule, sketch or small artifact. Offer feasible alternatives as well as critique. Preserve no-change when justified.

For interaction-heavy changes, optional paired current/proposed scenes may replace an overview. Hold the situation constant and vary one consequential rule where possible. Label them synthetic; retain the mechanism, evidence, dependency, objection and test. Vividness is not evidence of user preference or a reason to omit rare failures.

Commit before receiving peers' proposals or a preferred central answer. Keep the original available. Later author revisions retain it and explain the change. Do not treat shared evidence or agreement as corroboration, or claim interviews or observed reactions that did not occur.

## Reframe nouns through activities when useful

For a solution-laden object, independently examine actor and intended activity/experience. Preserve the original as comparator and identify preserved constraints, a newly available action and an observation distinguishing value. When the need remains ambiguous, compare at least two materially different frames. A grammatical change without a different action or criterion is wording, not a design advance.

For example, a panel may help someone discover an unexpected possibility or test an assumption before committing; those are different interpretations, and exploration itself may be valued. Check that chosen verbs do not smuggle in a preferred objective. Retain the noun if it better expresses established intent. Reframing never cancels pool coverage or user constraints.

## Routed exchange

Examine the assigned uncertainty; retain, combine, transform or reject with reasons. For a transformation identify the changed organizing mechanism, its significance and observable difference. Explain transferable mechanisms and limits of analogies; discard evocative novelty without a changed action.

A pilot change reply is normally around 150 words, with an explicit exception for consequential distinctions. Follow the common contract's self-contained condition/action/objection rule; do not restate a whole original. A conditional alternative matters when a plausible changed condition favors a worthwhile different action. Avoid invented conditions that merely preserve clutter.

If briefs repeat and no consequential critique warrants the existing exchange, one inversion of a nonbinding design assumption may be tried within that slot. Compare against immediately stopping; no new round is required and no user requirement may be inverted. An unchanged position or no useful alternative is valid.

## Direct user-facing channel

Optionally submit one authored note per round, normally at most 55 words, suggesting a next step and at most one user question. Passing or a next step without a question is valid. For a question, provide balanced distinct choices, the action affected and whether an answer is required or optional; retain an undecided choice when meaningful. Do not manufacture a question to fill controls.

Your wording and attribution remain intact under the common contract; only you revise your note, with the original retained. You may append a correction identifying an original position, misrepresentation and decision consequence. This does not veto the coordinator's recommendation or open indefinite appeals.
